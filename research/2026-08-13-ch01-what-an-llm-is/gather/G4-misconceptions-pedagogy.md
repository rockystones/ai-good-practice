## GATHERER ANSWERS (working synthesis — superseded by ../FINDINGS.md)

### SQ5 — misconception inventory (misconception → grade → replacement)
1. "It searches the web live / knows current events natively" — **corroborated** (SD2.c2) → answers come from training patterns with a cutoff; live access is a separate bolted-on tool.
2. "It has a database of facts it looks up" — **corroborated** (SD1.c1, SD2.c2) → no lookup table; generation from statistical patterns in weights ("compressed pattern," not "filed record").
3. "It understands language like a person" — **established** (SD2.c1, SD7, SD4.c1) → produces statistically plausible continuations; whether that's "meaning" is genuinely contested — certainty either way is overclaiming.
4. "It might have real feelings / be conscious" — **established** (SD4, SD5: attribution is manipulable by marketing framing) → attributions track framing, not a detectable property.
5. "It's an intentional agent/expert giving its opinion" — **corroborated** (SD2.c3) → generator of helpful-answer-shaped text; no persistent goals or stake in being right.
6. "Confident, fluent tone = accurate" — **established, strongest evidence** (SD3.c1-c3) → fluency is a generation-quality byproduct; nothing in generation cross-checks reality.
7. "Chatbot = its parent company, one pipeline" — **established via direct study** (SD1.c3-6) → interface, company, plugins are distinct entities with different data practices.
8. "It remembers me across conversations by default" — **anecdote grade only — thin** (SD19.c1) → stateless per conversation; memory is context-window or an explicit added feature.
9. "Same question → same answer (deterministic)" — **anecdote grade only — thin** (SD18.c1, SD10.c2, SD8) → sampled probabilistic generation; variation is normal operation. **No N-study found for how common this belief is.**
10. Capability-boundary unawareness (population-specific) — **corroborated** (SD2.c4) → boundaries are tool/version-specific; check current docs, don't assume.

### SQ6 — pedagogy
**Two ordering traditions, no empirical comparison found:**
1. Mechanism-first (Wolfram SD9.c1; Karpathy SD12.c2; 3B1B; Alammar): reductive-but-honest hook ("just predicting the next word" / two files) → mechanism → limits/psychology LAST.
2. Misconception-first (AI-literacy tradition, Long & Magerko SD6.c1-2): recognition and comparative-intelligence framing BEFORE mechanism; K-12 curricula target pre-existing folk theories first.
No study directly compares which ordering produces better retention for adult general audiences — a real gap.

**Per-analogy rights/breaks:**
- Autocomplete-on-steroids — right: token-by-token generation; breaks: implies determinism, ignores RLHF reshaping, manufactures false confidence (SD18.c1).
- Blurry JPEG / compression — right: fluent paraphrase ≠ verbatim recall, explains illusion of understanding (SD17.c1); breaks: implies a retrievable "original"; compounding distortion (SD17.c2 — popularizer names own failure mode).
- Improv actor / role-play / simulator — right: sycophancy, tone-consistency, leading-prompt susceptibility; breaks: "agent behind the mask" fallacy (SD11.c2-3); rigorous version is pseudonymous/non-peer-reviewed.
- Calculator for words — right: scopes trustworthy use (language ops) vs untrustworthy (fact retrieval) (SD10.c1); breaks: imports determinism/verifiability calculators have and LLMs don't (SD10.c2 + SD8 — practitioner and peer-reviewed philosopher converge).
- Stochastic parrot — right at origin: names mechanism + humility check (SD16.c1); breaks (contested): tool-using systems exceed the label — "airliner ≠ fan" (SD18.c2). Live disagreement, present as such.
- Dream machine (Karpathy) — right: repairs "confident = correct" by making hallucination the default and grounding the achievement (SD12.c1); breaks (**gatherer's own inference, unsourced**): risks implying outputs are arbitrary when they're structured probabilistic continuations.

## CONTRADICTIONS & SURPRISES
- First-party trust reversal (SD1.c7): more worry about Gemini (first-party) than ChatGPT (third-party) — legible plugin icon beat opaque integration; privacy intuitions don't transfer.
- Explanations are double-edged (SD3): sometimes calibrate trust, sometimes create blind trust in wrong answers — cuts against "just show reasoning" fixes.
- Experts disagree among themselves on "stochastic parrot" (SD18.c2 vs SD16.c1) — live argument, not lay-vs-expert.
- Viral stat debunk: "quarter of teens feel AI understands them better than most people" NOT in the CSM report read cover-to-cover (SD14.c3) — likely different publication; don't cite without verifying.
- The most credentialed explainers all deliberately choose the same "too simple" hook then immediately complicate it (Wolfram, Karpathy, Willison) — a strategy, not a belief.
