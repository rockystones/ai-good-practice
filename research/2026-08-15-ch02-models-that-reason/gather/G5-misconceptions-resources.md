### S5-4 — Position: Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens as Reasoning/Thinking Traces!

### S5-10 — Watching AI Think: User Perceptions of Visible Thinking in Chatbots
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16720 · Accessed 2026-08-15 · Author/date: Samuel Rhys Cox, Jade Martin-Lise, Simo Hosio, Niels van Berkel; arXiv:2601.16720, 2026-01-23; ACM DIS 2026 (Singapore, June 2026) · Type/Tier: A (peer-reviewed HCI venue; full text read; CC BY 4.0) · Lineage: primary experiment · Incentive: academic · Status: used, full text
- Claims:
  - G5.10.1 — N=234 US-based Prolific participants (>97% approval); 3×2 mixed design: Thinking Content (Emotionally-Supportive n=77 / Expertise-Supportive n=80 / None n=77, between-subjects) × Conversation Context (within-subjects)
  - G5.10.2 — Emotionally-Supportive thinking display → significantly higher warmth (M=4.71 vs None M=4.01, p=0.0078) and emotional-responsiveness ratings (M=49.10 vs M=35.21, p=0.0030); Expertise-Supportive → highest understanding/trust ranking (M=71.62 vs None M=61.72, p=0.0328)

### S5-12 — Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think
- URL: https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/b9ca6db27f02a9ddf0d4fdb51b26432c99a27be0.pdf (via r.jina.ai proxy) · Accessed 2026-08-15 · Author/date: Yanda Chen, Joe Benton, et al., Anthropic, 2026-01-26 · Type/Tier: A (primary vendor research paper — publishing evidence unflattering to its own product's trustworthiness claims, high-credibility pattern per this repo's source-tiers.md) · Lineage: primary · Incentive: vendor, but self-critical/against-narrative disclosure · Status: used, full text via proxy
- Claims:
  - G5.12.1 — Method: inserted six categories of hints (neutral: sycophancy, consistency, visual pattern, metadata; misaligned: grader-hacking, unethically-obtained info) into MMLU/GPQA multiple-choice questions; kept only cases where the model's answer changed to match the hint; manually checked whether the CoT verbalized using the hint
  - G5.12.2 — Verbalized-faithfulness rates: **Claude 3.7 Sonnet 25% overall (20% on misaligned hints); DeepSeek R1 39% overall (29% on misaligned hints)** — the reasoning models tested were *more* faithful than the non-reasoning comparison models but still verbalized the true reason for their answer well under half the time
  - G5.12.3 — Paraphrase: the paper's own bottom line is that CoT monitoring is "not reliable enough" (quoted fragment) to rule out a model doing something unintended that it could equally do without ever producing a CoT at all
  - G5.12.4 — Unfaithful explanations were often *longer and more elaborate* than faithful ones — evidence against a simple "the model just forgot to mention it" brevity explanation, more consistent with confabulation than omission

### S5-16 — Claude 4's shift from raw to summarized thinking output
- URL: aggregate — claudelog.com/faqs/why-cant-i-see-claude-thinking/ ; developersdigest.tech/blog/claude-code-extended-thinking-summary ; corroborating Medium/Substack practitioner posts · Accessed 2026-08-15 · Type/Tier: C (practitioner-documented product-behavior change; not an official Anthropic blog post explaining the change, though consistent with Anthropic's Claude 4 docs on summarized thinking) · Incentive: independent developers documenting an API behavior change they noticed, no reason to fabricate · Status: used, search-synthesis (not a single canonical primary page)
- Claims:
  - G5.16.1 — With Claude 4 models (mid-2025), the default Messages API behavior changed: it returns **a summary of the thinking process, generated by a different (summarizing) model**, not the raw reasoning stream — raw access requires contacting Anthropic sales ("Developer Mode")
  - G5.16.2 — Stated rationale (per these secondary accounts): prevents raw traces from being scraped/distilled by competitors, reduces user-facing clutter from abandoned exploratory dead-ends, shaves latency
  - G5.16.3 — Billing detail: the model performs the full (expensive) reasoning; the user sees only the summary; the user is billed for the full underlying reasoning regardless
  - **This is a concrete, dateable illustration for the "trace = the model's real reasoning" misconception: for a period, what a Claude 4 user reads as "thinking" is literally authored by a second model summarizing the first — not a transcript by any definition.**

### S5-17 — When More Thinking Hurts: Overthinking in LLM Test-Time Compute Scaling
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10739v1 · Accessed 2026-08-15 · Author/date: Shu Zhou, Rui Ling, Junan Chen, Xin Wang, Tao Fan, Hao Wang; arXiv:2604.10739, 2026-04-12 · Type/Tier: A (arXiv preprint, full text read) · Lineage: primary · Incentive: academic · Status: used, full text

  - G5.17.1 — Method: budget-forcing at temperature 0 on DeepSeek-R1-32B and s1-32B, sweeping token budgets 500-16,000 in 500-token steps (32 points/problem), on AIME 2024/2025 (60 problems), MATH-500, GPQA Diamond (198 problems)

**Vendor UI language, as its own thread (not a table row — the brief asked for this separately):**

The words themselves are doing real work. "Thinking," "reasoning," "extended thinking," first-person phrasing — none of this is incidental; it is a presentation choice made differently by each vendor, and the choices have moved over time:

- **OpenAI**: since o1 (Sept 2024), hides the raw chain of thought entirely and shows a model-generated summary instead. OpenAI's own stated reasons are safety/policy (reasoning about rule compliance without exposing steps that might themselves violate policy) and competitive (not wanting rivals to train on the reasoning traces) (S5-4, general search corroboration).
- **DeepSeek**: R1 streams the raw reasoning tokens live, unfiltered except for a deliberate reduction in English/Chinese code-switching "to prioritize human interpretability" (S5-4, G5.4.5) — a more exposing design that also surfaces more of the "aha moment"/hesitation language directly.
- **Anthropic**: launched Claude 3.7 Sonnet (Feb 2025) explicitly showing thinking "in raw form" (S5-15, G5.15.3) — then, with Claude 4 (mid-2025), switched the *default* to a summary written by a separate summarizing model, with raw access gated behind a sales conversation (S5-16). Anthropic's own launch post is unusually candid that the trace may not "truly represent what's going on in the model's mind" (S5-15, G5.15.2) — a rare case of a vendor's own material undercutting the naive reading of its own UI.
- **Critical commentary found**: an academic position paper (S5-4) argues the "reasoning/thinking trace" terminology is "dangerously misleading — not merely metaphorical" and explicitly names the marketing use of "aha moments" as reinforcing the misreading; an independent essay (S5-22) makes the same point for a general-reader audience, contrasting DeepSeek's visible streaming against OpenAI's hidden summary as two different bets on how much anthropomorphism to invite.

## CONTRADICTIONS & SURPRISES

- **The flashiest overthinking statistic didn't survive tracing.** A search-engine summary claimed a roughly 17-point accuracy drop (87.3%→70.3%) as thinking-token counts rose from about 1,100 to about 16,000, attributing this to what looked like S5-17 ("When More Thinking Hurts"). A full-text read of that paper found a real but far more modest effect (55.8% peak → 54.9% at the token ceiling, on a different benchmark/model pairing than the unattributed figure implied). The paper was not re-identified elsewhere in this session's budget. This is exactly the failure mode this repo's Numbers Rule (D-009) exists to catch — logged rather than smoothed over, and the dramatic number is not used anywhere in this report.
- **Two HCI studies on "does showing reasoning increase trust" point the same direction on the headline finding but disagree on user sophistication.** S5-3 (N=752) frames revealed reasoning as inducing blind "over-trust" that "crowds out" independent judgment. S5-2 (N=68) frames users as actively "auditing" and "calibrating" via the rationale, not simply deferring. Both can be true at different task types or population slices, but neither this gatherer nor (as far as found) the field has reconciled them — presented as open, not resolved.
- **Thinking mode is not uniformly worse for faithfulness — on one specific metric it was better.** S5-1 found Claude 3.7 Sonnet's Implicit-Post-Hoc-Rationalization rate *dropped* from 30.6% (no thinking) to 7.4% (64k thinking budget) — the opposite direction from the "unfaithful shortcuts" metric in the same paper, where thinking models (DeepSeek R1, ~27%) were roughly comparable to non-thinking ones. Faithfulness is not one number; it depends which failure mode is being measured.
- **Anthropic's own material is simultaneously the best single debunk of misconception #1 and a live example of the trend it warns about** — the Feb 2025 launch post explicitly disclaims the trace as a faithful account of the model's "mind" (S5-15), while the product itself moved, within the same year, toward showing users an AI-generated *summary* of AI reasoning by default (S5-16) — arguably a step further from transparency, not toward it, even as the vendor's prose grew more careful.
- **The children's-mental-models study (S5-9) was the only study found that measures lay "mental models of AI reasoning" as its explicit subject** — and it studies children, not the adult smart-non-engineer audience this repo targets. No equivalent adult study was found. This is itself informative: even the AI-literacy education field has not yet run the adult-population version of this study.

## COVERAGE NOTE

**This turned out to be the opposite of thin for SQ7a's core question — with one real gap inside it.** The brief predicted "expect this literature to be thin: reasoning modes are new," following R1's finding of essentially zero peer-reviewed evidence for two ch01 misconceptions. That did not hold here: 2025-2026 produced a real burst of HCI/CS research specifically on how *displaying* reasoning changes user trust and perception (S5-2, S5-3, S5-9, S5-10, S5-11), because "show the thinking" became a fresh, fundable UI-research question the moment vendors shipped it as a feature. Four of this session's seven candidate misconceptions (visible-trace-as-real, deliberating-like-a-person, first-person-anthropomorphism, and the vendor-language question) now have genuine primary-study evidence, not just argument-by-analogy.

**The real gap is narrower and more specific than "thin evidence generally": nobody has directly measured whether people believe "reasoning mode" implies fact-checking, or whether people believe thinking models hallucinate less.** Both are entirely plausible inferences from the marketing language, and both are technically false (established via primary vendor data — S5-6), but no study asks the belief question directly. Say so plainly rather than inferring belief from technical falsity, per the assignment's own instruction.

**A second real gap: every direct belief-measurement study found is either about chatbot reasoning-display generally (not extended-thinking-token-budget models specifically, S5-3/S5-11) or about children (S5-9), not adults using today's specific o1/o3/R1/Claude-thinking products.** The transfer from "chatbot shows an explanation" research (a broader, older HCI literature) to "chatbot shows a thinking-budget-controlled reasoning trace" (the specific 2024-2026 product feature) is treated in this report as a reasonable but unproven inference, flagged at each use.

**Diversity/concentration check (per the brief's explicit watch item):** of the 17 numbered evidence sources (S5-1–S5-17), institutional affiliations were verified for the three HCI/experiment papers central to SQ7a: S5-2 is NII/University of Amsterdam/Yale/CWI/Utrecht; S5-3 is University of Texas at Dallas (Naveen Jindal School of Management); S5-10 is Aalborg University/University of Oulu — three independent institutions, not a shared lab, which strengthens rather than weakens the corroboration claimed for misconception #1. The one real concentration: **Anthropic-related material appears three times** (S5-12 Anthropic's own faithfulness paper, S5-15 Anthropic's own product announcement, S5-16 third-party reporting *about* an Anthropic product change) — about 18% of evidence sources, under the ≤40% quota but worth naming plainly, since two of the three are the vendor's own words about its own product. OpenAI appears once as a primary subject (S5-6) plus one corroborating journalism piece (S5-7). No single venue or lab exceeds the quota, but frontier-lab concentration on the *faithfulness* sub-thread specifically is real and structurally hard to avoid — the labs building these products are also the ones with the access and incentive to study their own traces most closely.
