## ANSWERS

### SQ4 — where it helps (with numbers + their exact conditions)

**Competition math (AIME 2024, pass@1 unless noted).** DeepSeek-R1 vs its own non-reasoning base DeepSeek-V3: 39.2% → 79.8% (G3.1.1, primary table). The pure-RL DeepSeek-R1-Zero variant rose from 15.6% to 71.0% over training, 86.7% with majority-vote@64 (G3.1.3) — showing the gain tracks the RL training process itself. OpenAI o1-preview vs GPT-4o: 13% → 83% (G3.2.1, **access-caveated**, not independently traced to primary). Claude 3.7 Sonnet standard vs extended thinking (64K-token high-compute setting): 23.3% → 80.0% (G3.5.1, **access-caveated**). MATH-500: DeepSeek 90.2% → 97.3% (G3.1.1, primary).

**Science QA (GPQA Diamond).** DeepSeek-R1 vs V3: 59.1% → 71.5% (G3.1.1, primary). Claude standard vs extended thinking: 68.0% → 84.8% (G3.5.1, access-caveated). o1 described as exceeding PhD-level human accuracy vs GPT-4o (G3.2.1, access-caveated).

**Code.** DeepSeek-R1 vs V3, Codeforces rating: 1134 → 2029, percentile 58.7% → 96.3% (G3.1.1, primary). LiveCodeBench pass@1-COT: 36.2% → 65.9% (primary). SWE-bench Verified (resolved): 42.0% → 49.2%; Aider-Polyglot: 16.0% → 53.3% (G3.1.2, primary — genuine agentic-coding pair). o1 vs GPT-4o Codeforces: 808 Elo/11th percentile → 1807 Elo/89th percentile (G3.2.1, access-caveated).

**Planning.** The cleanest, most fully primary-verified result in this file: on the *identical* 600-instance standard PlanBench Blocksworld benchmark, prior non-reasoning models scored GPT-4 34.6%, GPT-4o 35.5%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 54.8%, LLaMA 3.1 405B 62.6%, and o1-preview scored **97.8%** (G3.4.1-2, both from the same paper's Tables 1-2 — same benchmark, same scoring, directly comparable). o1-mini, despite also being "a reasoning model," scored only 56.6% on the same task — reasoning-branding does not guarantee a uniform gain even within one vendor's lineup.

**Abstract visual reasoning (ARC-AGI-1).** GPT-4o (non-reasoning) ≈5% (2024) → o1 (reasoning) ≈32% max → o3-preview (reasoning, Dec 2024, high-compute) 87.5% (G3.3.1, primary, but see the vintage-sensitivity caveat under CONTRADICTIONS).

**Agentic/tool tasks.** SWE-bench and Aider-Polyglot numbers above. Separately, at the level of a frontier-wide trend rather than a single paired comparison: task-completion time-horizons grow roughly 2× faster in doubling-time terms when models can externalize reasoning (≈182-day doubling) versus when chain-of-thought is suppressed (≈373-day doubling) (G3.7.1) — a rate-of-improvement claim, not a same-day accuracy delta.

### SQ4 — where it doesn't help or hurts

**Fragility to surface changes on grade-school math.** GSM-Symbolic: renaming entities produces some variance, but changing only numerical values produces more (G3.11.2). Adding one logically-irrelevant-but-plausible clause (GSM-NoOp) causes performance drops of "up to 65%... across all state-of-the-art models" (G3.11.1, verbatim from abstract) even though the clause contributes nothing to the needed reasoning chain.

**Complexity-threshold collapse on controlled puzzles.** Apple's Illusion of Thinking: at low complexity, non-thinking models match or beat thinking models; only at medium complexity does thinking clearly help; at high complexity both collapse toward zero (G3.8.2). Collapse arrives at Tower-of-Hanoi N≈7-8 (~100-200 moves) and River-Crossing N=3 (an 11-move solution) (G3.8.3) — notably low absolute complexity for the latter. Near the threshold, models paradoxically *reduce* reasoning-token spend despite the task getting harder and budget remaining (G3.8.4) — this effort-reduction pattern, not the raw collapse, is the paper's more distinctive claim.

**Purpose-built inverse-scaling tasks.** Anthropic's self-authored Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute finds several current frontier models (Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 3.7/4, o3-mini, o4-mini, o3, DeepSeek R1, Qwen3-32B, QwQ-32B) losing accuracy as reasoning is extended on tasks built with distractors, spurious regression features, and constraint-tracking deduction (G3.14.2-3). Claude Opus 4 on one such task falls from near-100% to roughly 85-90% (G3.14.3, approximate range). This is self-critical, published-against-interest evidence (Anthropic testing its own models) — but the tasks are adversarially constructed for this purpose, so generalization to ordinary task distributions is untested by this paper (G3.14.5).

**Overthinking on ordinary competition math, at smaller model scale.** "When More Thinking Hurts" finds AIME accuracy on 32B-scale reasoning models peaking around 12K tokens (55.8%) then declining to 54.9% by 16K, with a "flip ratio" of correct-to-incorrect answer changes turning net-negative from ~7K tokens onward (G3.15.2-3). Direction corroborates Anthropic's frontier-scale finding; the specific magnitude is a small/distilled-model result, not a frontier number (G3.15.4).

**Thinking vs not-thinking on the SAME reasoning-trained model, at matched budgets.** "Reasoning Models Can Be Effective Without Thinking": on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, skipping the visible thinking step matches or beats explicit thinking under matched (often tight) token budgets — e.g. AMC 2023 at 700 tokens: 51.3% (no-think) vs 28.9% (think) (G3.16.2); AIME 2024 at 3500 tokens: 77.30% vs 73.33% (G3.16.3). **This is a different axis from every "reasoning model beats non-reasoning sibling" number above** — same model, thinking toggled, budget held fixed — not reasoning-trained vs never-trained-to-reason. Conflating the two would misrepresent both findings.

**Where thinking adds ~nothing by task type (qualitative, not independently number-verified by this gatherer).** Multiple 2025-2026 papers surfaced by search (OptimalThinkingBench, structural-overthinking studies) converge on: thinking's benefit shrinks or reverses on tasks a non-reasoning model already solves reliably (simple factual retrieval, short style/writing tasks), and shrinks once base model scale exceeds roughly 4-8B parameters. These were not individually primary-fetched by this gatherer (budget triage — the direction was consistent across several independent search hits and consistent with G3.16's matched-budget finding) — mark as corroborated-but-not-table-traced, direction only.

**Reliability failures distinct from raw accuracy.** o1 and R1 "saturate" static solvable Blocksworld but, on *unsolvable* instances, generate confident, elaborate, false justifications rather than reporting impossibility (G3.12.4) — a trust/calibration failure that a pure accuracy percentage does not capture.

**Chain-of-thought prompting gains are brittle/overfit, not generally algorithmic.** Pre-dating the current reasoning-model wave, but foundational to the critique: CoT-prompted gains on planning "quickly deteriorate" once problem size exceeds what worked examples showed (G3.13.1), and zero-shot GPT-4 on one Blocksworld variant scored as low as 3.83% (G3.13.2) — see the Numbers Rule case study below.

### Brittleness

**Renaming/obfuscation.** Standard-to-Mystery Blocksworld (syntactic obfuscation, identical underlying logic): prior non-reasoning models fall to under 5%; o1-preview holds at 52.8%, down from its own 97.8% on the unobfuscated version (G3.4.3) — partial resistance, not immunity. GSM-Symbolic shows the same family of fragility on math word problems under numeric perturbation (G3.11.1-2).

**Longer chains / plan length.** o1-preview: 97.8% on the full mixed-length Blocksworld set, 23.63% specifically on the 20-40-step-optimal-plan subset (G3.4.4) — independently confirms the figure supplied in my assignment brief.

**Distribution shift / complexity threshold, and how contested the *interpretation* is.** Apple's collapse points (G3.8.3) are disputed on two, not one, fronts, and the disputes don't land the same way on both puzzle families:
- The Lawsen/C.Opus rebuttal (G3.9) argues much of the River-Crossing collapse specifically is a scoring artifact: some tested instances (N≥6 agents, 3-capacity boat) are mathematically unsolvable yet scored as failures (G3.9.3), and reformatting Hanoi as code-generation rather than move-listing restores "very high accuracy" at N=15 (G3.9.4).
- The independent third replication (G3.10, Dellibarda Varela et al., using Gemini 2.5 Pro) does **not** simply side with either prior paper: it confirms the River-Crossing critique — solvable large instances are "effortlessly" solved once unsolvable ones are excluded (G3.10.3) — but *also* confirms a real Hanoi degradation around 8 disks that survives controlling for output-window effects, describing it as a genuine cognition limit (G3.10.2).
- **Net for a reader**: brittleness under complexity/distribution shift is real for at least Tower of Hanoi even by the most careful independent check found; the equally-famous River Crossing "collapse" looks substantially like a measurement artifact. Treat the two puzzle families as separate evidence, not a single data point.

**A Numbers Rule case study worth carrying into the chapter.** My brief supplied "97.8% vs ~3% for 2023-era models" on Blocksworld. I independently confirmed 97.8% exactly (G3.4.1) — but on the *same* standard PlanBench benchmark, 2023-era GPT-4 actually scored 34.6%, not ~3% (G3.4.2). The most plausible true source of "~3%" is a *different* paper by the same research group, on a differently-formatted "Table-to-Stack" Blocksworld task, zero-shot condition: GPT-4 3.83% (G3.13.2-3). The 97.8% and the ~3% figures almost certainly come from two different benchmarks under two different prompting conditions, not one apples-to-apples comparison. Both individual numbers are real and traced; the *pairing* is very likely a cross-benchmark composite. This should not ship as a single "before/after" claim.

### Debate state as of mid-2026 (who argues what; what would move it)

**Skeptical/deflationary camp.** Melanie Mitchell (Santa Fe Institute): traces aren't reliably faithful and are often prunable without loss (G3.17.1). Subbarao Kambhampati (ASU) and his group (G3.4, G3.12, G3.13, all directly verified by this gatherer, not secondhand): reasoning tokens reflect "iteratively compiling reasoning into retrieval" (G3.12.1) rather than symbolic reasoning; CoT gains are prompt-specific and don't generalize (G3.13.1); models hallucinate false certainty on unsolvable problems (G3.12.4). Apple's Shojaee/Mirzadeh team stands by the original complexity-collapse framing as of publication (G3.8).

**Defending/functionalist camp.** Sébastien Bubeck (OpenAI, per G3.17.3) argues the model "reason[s] like a human would" and characterizes some critiques as targeting already-superseded ("obsolete") models/methods. The Lawsen/C.Opus rebuttal (G3.9) — notable partly *because* a model is credited as its lead-positioned co-author, an unresolved norms question the field hasn't settled — argues the strongest deflationary evidence is substantially a measurement artifact.

**Middle position, and the most careful single check available.** Dellibarda Varela et al. (G3.10) explicitly decline to fully back either side: some collapse is real (Hanoi), some is artifact (River Crossing) — "today's LRMs are stochastic, RL-tuned searchers" (5-word trim of a longer quote) rather than either symbolic reasoners or pure illusions.

**What's newer in 2026, reported secondhand via Quanta and not independently opened here** (G3.17.4): capability-side evidence cited by defenders includes an OpenAI contribution (reported May 2026) to an open math problem and a DeepMind/Terence Tao collaboration on 67 math problems; skeptic-side evidence cited includes studies reporting 30-60% of reasoning steps carry "minimal causal impact" on the final answer. These sit closer to SQ3 (faithfulness) than SQ4 and are flagged for that gatherer/verifier rather than chased here.

**What would move it**, synthesizing across sources: (1) a falsifiable, pre-committed definition of "genuine reasoning" that both camps would accept before seeing results — none of my sources offer one, echoing the same gap the prior session found in the parallel emergent-abilities/world-model debate; (2) more independent third-party replications in the style of G3.10 across additional puzzle/task families, since only Hanoi and River Crossing have had a serious independent check so far; (3) evidence on whether inverse-scaling/overthinking effects generalize from adversarially-constructed suites (G3.14) to naturally-occurring task distributions.

**Against the brief's pre-registered priors**: this evidence is consistent with **P4 holding** (genuinely contested, credible people on both sides — not falsified) and **P5 holding** (strong attacks have strong rebuttals; neither side has cleanly won, and the most careful third check splits the difference rather than resolving it). Consistent also with **P2** (task-specificity: gains concentrate in math/code/planning/science-QA and shrink or invert on simple/adversarially-constructed tasks) — not falsified by anything found here.

## CONTRADICTIONS & SURPRISES

- **The "~3%" figure in my assignment does not trace to the same benchmark as the 97.8% figure it was paired with** — see the Numbers Rule case study above. Flagging this as the single most important correction in this file.
- **ARC-AGI's "87.5%" is date-and-version-sensitive in a way that bit even the benchmark's own organizers**: the headline Dec-2024 o3-preview number (75.7%/87.5% at low/high compute) is explicitly *not* comparable, per ARC Prize's own later post, to the production o3 released months later, which scored lower (41/53/82%) on close variants of the same eval. A live demonstration of the access-notes/protocol warning that benchmark numbers age in months.
- **The rebuttal paper's byline is itself a data point**: "C. Opus, Anthropic" appears lead-positioned ahead of the human co-author on a paper arguing reasoning models reason better than a rival lab's paper gave them credit for — self-referential in a way the field has not developed citation/authorship norms for yet. Noted as a fact about the paper, not evaluated further.
- **The two Apple-vs-rebuttal puzzle families resolve in opposite directions** under the most careful independent check (G3.10): Hanoi's collapse looks real; River Crossing's looks like a scoring artifact. "The rebuttal debunked Apple" and "Apple was basically right" are both oversimplifications — accuracy depends on which puzzle you mean.
- **Within one research group's own body of work**, o1/R1 "saturating" solvable Blocksworld (G3.4, G3.12) sits alongside the same group's finding that the same models confidently fabricate plans for *unsolvable* instances rather than admitting impossibility (G3.12.4) — high accuracy and unreliable calibration coexist in the same models on the same benchmark family.
- **"NoThinking beats Thinking" (G3.16) and "thinking-trained beats never-trained-to-reason" (G3.1, G3.4, G3.2) sound contradictory but are not** — they compare different things (thinking on/off within one reasoning-trained model at a matched budget, vs. a reasoning-trained model against a model never trained to reason, typically at unconstrained budget). Flagged explicitly since this is an easy misread for a non-engineer reader, and precisely the kind of distinction a primer chapter should preserve rather than smooth over.
- **Apple appears on both the "against" and (indirectly, as the subject of independent replication) the debate-state list three times** (Illusion of Thinking, GSM-Symbolic, and as the target of two rebuttal papers) — two of the three AGAINST-side arXiv papers I used share two authors (Mirzadeh, Farajtabar) with a third (Illusion of Thinking and GSM-Symbolic overlap), meaning the AGAINST case rests on fewer fully-independent lineages than the raw source count suggests. Noted for the diversity/independence check.
