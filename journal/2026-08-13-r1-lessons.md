# 2026-08-13 — Lessons from R1 (the pilot research session)

**Context.** First full run of the research protocol: Chapter 01, five parallel gatherers + two adversarial verifiers. Findings in `research/2026-08-13-ch01-what-an-llm-is/`.

**The big lesson — numbers detach from their labels.** Four of the eight anchor claims we sent to adversarial verification came back corrected, and every correction was the same species of error: a real number separated from what it actually measured by a summary layer in between. Two hallucination metrics of one model read as two models; a share of coded interview sentences (26 of 28) presented as "93% of participants" — *by a peer-reviewed systematic review misreading its own source*; an abstract's "up to 99%" untraceable to any results table; a 2023 "3%" quoted as if current when the same lab's 2024 number is 97.8%. None fabricated. All wrong. → Now the **Numbers Rule** (D-009, in the protocol): no statistic ships without tracing it to the primary table it came from.

**Verification is where the budget earned its keep.** Five Sonnet gatherers produced a wide, honest sweep — including flagging their own weak spots — but the two Opus verifiers changed the answers. A gate you never test with negative cases passes everything (P-001); a research pipeline without an adversarial pass is the same gate. The tier design (gather wide and cheap, verify narrow and hard) is validated and stays.

**Sources about to be trusted, caught in time.** A rebuttal paper we nearly featured as the counter-position on hallucination turned out to be a one-citation preprint with an unidentifiable co-author and a product-shaped conclusion — replaced by a stronger, honest pairing found during verification. Loud-because-convenient is a real trap.

**Smaller lessons.** Vendor documents that admit something against interest (GPT-4's own report: "post-training hurts calibration significantly") are disproportionately valuable. Official docs contain deployment truths no explainer mentions (temperature 0 isn't deterministic). Verify names: two different Mitchells (Margaret, Melanie) hold different positions in the same debate. A viral statistic ("a quarter of teens feel AI understands them better than people") isn't in the report it's attributed to.

**Candidate practice card** for the next distill session: "Trace the number to its table" (the Numbers Rule as a general habit — applies to journalism, meeting decks, and AI-generated summaries alike, not just our research).
