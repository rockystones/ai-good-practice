# P-002 — Pre-register the question before searching

**One-liner.** Write down what question you're answering — and what would change your mind — before you type the first query.

**When.** Any research session, with or without AI: web searching, deep-research runs, literature reads, and every "let me just look this up" that will inform a real decision.

**Do.**

- Write the question in one sentence, plus the 3–7 sub-questions a complete answer must cover.
- Note your prior: what you currently believe, and what evidence would change it.
- Define what "done" looks like — coverage, not hours — before starting.
- If you notice mid-search that the real question is different: that's a finding. Revise the brief explicitly; don't drift silently.

**Because.** Unframed search optimizes for what is findable, not what you need. You end up answering a different, easier question, over-weighting whatever the loudest sources discuss, and using results to rationalize what you already believed. Writing the falsifier down first is the cheapest known defense against confirmation bias.

**Failure signs.** The session ends somewhere unrelated to any question you could state. Every source you kept agrees with you. You can't say what would have changed your mind.

**Boundaries.** Simple fact lookups ("what year did X ship") don't need a brief. The trigger is: the answer informs a decision, or you expect to synthesize across sources.

**Provenance.** Experience, plus standard practice in empirical research (pre-registration). Operationalized as Phase 0 of [method/research-protocol.md](../method/research-protocol.md).
